## 1. Why the roadmap changed

The former roadmap was technically careful but too ceremonial for the available time and the scale of a school club with roughly 20 members. Individual phases were taking about three hours, browser automation consumed disproportionate model usage, and low-priority infrastructure was delaying the public recruitment experience.

The compact roadmap prioritizes the product outcome that matters now: a student follows the poster link, stays interested, completes a straightforward application, proves that they control a TIS Google account, and gives leadership usable application data.

This change reduces sequencing and implementation overhead. It does not discard the established security foundation or excuse careless production launch behavior.

## 2. Approved product priorities

In order:

1. Persuasive, accessible public landing page.
2. Simple student application journey.
3. Google identity as a TIS identification step, not as an invitation into an empty dashboard.
4. Clear leadership access to applications through a protected view and CSV export.
5. Essential membership and attendance operations.
6. Public-site completion, recovery proof, security/accessibility checks, and launch.
7. Member dashboards and deep Workspace automation only when they become useful.

## 4. Deliberate compromises

### Experience

- The landing and application experience receives the strongest design attention.
- The student dashboard remains structural and barebones.
- Leadership receives focused task pages rather than a large generalized dashboard.
- Authentic content and usability matter more than decorative animation or dashboard density.

### Authentication

- Applicants still use Google identity.
- Sign-in only establishes identity and supported TIS affiliation evidence.
- Authentication does not create membership, grant protected access, or require an applicant dashboard.
- The preferred journey is landing page → form → Google verification → submission confirmation.

### Workspace

- Classroom and Drive use approved configured links when links solve the actual need.
- Drive browsing, permission management, uploads, and file copying are not launch requirements.
- Classroom API automation and roster synchronization remain excluded.
- Calendar API behavior and Gmail activation are used only where their benefit justifies provider setup.
- Live provider work from Phase 05 is not allowed to reopen or block the compact roadmap by default.

### Operations

- Membership and attendance retain correct data boundaries but use small, direct interfaces.
- Warning records may be deliberate and manual rather than generated by a complex policy engine.
- Reporting is limited to useful lists, details, totals, and CSV export.
- Existing Forms, Sheets, and responses are preserved rather than rushed through a one-day migration.

### Verification and agent usage

- Run focused checks while changing code and the full required suite once after changes settle.
- Perform one focused review instead of repeated model audits.
- Use Gemini for browser controls when safe and available.
- Otherwise ask the user to perform exact click-by-click steps.
- Do not spend large Codex sessions repeatedly navigating provider consoles or unchanged browser paths.
- A provider defect is documented and deferred unless it blocks the current critical journey.

## 5. What was not compromised

The compact roadmap does not weaken:

- verified school identity evidence;
- default-deny authorization;
- separation of authentication, affiliation, membership, and capability;
- protection of student information and secrets;
- boundary validation and safe errors;
- audit history for protected changes;
- production/non-production separation;
- preservation of legacy records;
- accessibility of critical journeys;
- a practical backup and restore proof before real production data;
- explicit approval for destructive, provider, account, merge, and launch actions.

## 6. Phase 06 application decisions

The exact form must be approved once before implementation. The baseline is intentionally short:

1. preferred name;
2. grade/year;
3. mathematical topics or activities of interest;
4. a short reason for joining LOGOS;
5. a short learning or contribution goal;
6. optional relevant experience, with no experience required;
7. ability to attend the normal meeting time;
8. confirmation that the information is accurate and may be used for club administration.

The form should avoid unnecessary personal or sensitive data. Each field needs explicit required/optional status, choices where applicable, and a modest response limit.

Leadership accesses submissions through `/admin/applications`, protected by current server-side capability checks. The minimum interface includes a list, readable details, review status, and CSV export. Direct Neon-console access is not part of normal club operation.

## 7. Phase 07 launch interpretation

Phase 07 is deliberately broad in outcome but shallow in unnecessary machinery. It uses four internal milestones—operations, minimal protected structure, public completion, and launch—without creating more numbered phase ceremonies.

An internal milestone may have focused commits and tests, but it does not require a separate roadmap rewrite, branch, pull request, repeated audit, or implementation session unless a real blocker makes separation necessary.

The launch decision should ask: “Can students apply, can leadership safely use the data, can the club perform its essential work, and can the system be operated and recovered?” It should not ask whether every conceivable dashboard or Google automation has been built.

## 8. Deferred enhancement list

After launch, add these only when demonstrated use justifies them:

- a richer member dashboard;
- deeper Drive discovery or previews;
- automated Classroom roster or membership behavior, if separately approved;
- Calendar editing through LOGOS Web;
- sophisticated warning automation and reporting;
- broader operational automation beyond the launch recovery baseline;
- additional visual or motion polish beyond the approved restrained design.

Deferral means the item is intentionally outside the one-day critical path. It does not prevent a later conventional enhancement proposal.
